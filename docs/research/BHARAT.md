# Bharat context

Why Siidekick targets small Indian web teams. Every claim ends with a footnote. Figures are quoted as the source gives them. Anything I could not source is left out (see the last section).

## Scale: small businesses online

- More than 7.28 crore MSMEs are registered on the Udyam portal, which launched on 1 July 2020. This counts registrations, not websites. [1]
- Internet users in India reached 886 million in 2024, of which 488 million are in rural India. [8]

## Cyber incidents

- CERT-In tracked 15,92,917 incidents in 2023, up from 13,91,457 in 2022. Secondary source: a news report of a Rajya Sabha reply. [2]
- CERT-In handled over 29.44 lakh incidents in 2025. [3]
- NCRB recorded 65,893 cyber crime cases in 2022, up 24.4% from 52,974 in 2021. Secondary source: a news report of the NCRB release. [4]
- Fraud was the motive in 42,710 of those 65,893 cases (64.8%). [4]
- These are national counts. None of these sources says how many incidents hit small websites. Siidekick makes no such claim.

## Law: DPDP Act 2023 and Rules 2025

- The DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified on 14 November 2025. [5]
- The Rules set an 18-month period for phased compliance. [5]
- The highest penalty, up to Rs 250 crore, applies to failure of a Data Fiduciary to maintain reasonable security safeguards. Failing to notify a breach can attract up to Rs 200 crore. [5]
- After a breach, the Data Fiduciary must tell affected individuals without delay, in plain language, with contact details for help. [5]
- Rule 6 lists minimum safeguards: encryption, obfuscation or masking; access control; logs and monitoring; and keeping logs and personal data for one year. The text was read on a third-party site, not the Gazette. [6]
- CERT-In Directions of 28 April 2022 say service providers, intermediaries, data centres and body corporate must report listed incidents to CERT-In within 6 hours of noticing them, and keep logs for a rolling 180 days. This is a 2022 document. [7]
- Whether a given club or small shop counts as a Data Fiduciary, or is exempt, is a legal question. These sources do not settle it, and Siidekick does not give legal advice.

## Language

- 870 million internet users (98%) accessed the internet in Indic languages in 2024. [8]
- 57% of internet users said they prefer Indic languages for content, but the report states this for urban India only. [8]
- Hindi was the mother tongue of 43.63% of Indians in the 2011 Census (2011 data), up from 36.99% in 1971. [9]
